Glass recycling in India

How waste glass becomes raw material again, and where Rian Cullet fits in that chain, from Delhi since 1996.

Glass is one of the few materials that can be recycled again and again without losing quality. A bottle melted down becomes a new bottle, as clear and as strong as the first. The material that makes this possible is cullet: recovered glass, sorted and processed so a furnace can use it.

Rian Cullet is a glass recycling company based in Delhi. Since 1996 we have collected, sorted and processed waste glass and supplied it as cullet to glass manufacturers in more than 22 states.

01

How the chain works

  1. Generation: glass is used and discarded by households, hotels, bars, bottlers and factories.
  2. Collection: in India much of it is gathered by kabadiwalas, scrap dealers and aggregators, and some by waste management operators.
  3. Sorting and processing: a cullet processor such as Rian Cullet sorts it by type and colour, removes contamination and prepares it for the furnace.
  4. Remelting: glass manufacturers add the cullet to their batch and melt it into new glass.
02

Why it matters

Cullet melts at a lower temperature than the sand, soda ash and limestone it replaces. Every tonne used means less raw material quarried, less fuel burned in the furnace and less CO2 released, both from the fuel and from the carbonates in the raw batch.

Industry sources commonly put the energy saving at roughly 2 to 3 percent for every 10 percent of cullet in the batch. For a glass plant that is a real cost saving; for a city it is glass kept out of landfill.

03

What makes recycled glass usable

Recycling glass well is mostly about sorting. A furnace needs cullet of the right colour and type, free of anything that will not melt cleanly:

  • Colour sorted into clear (flint), amber and green.
  • Container glass kept apart from flat glass.
  • Ceramics, stone and porcelain removed.
  • Metals, labels and organic residue removed.
  • Heat-resistant glass, bulbs and screens kept out.

Questions

Frequently asked

Is glass 100 percent recyclable?

Container glass can be remelted repeatedly without loss of quality, so in principle it is fully recyclable. In practice what limits it is collection and contamination, which is why sorting matters.

Why is recycled glass called cullet?

Cullet is the glass industry's term for broken or waste glass prepared for remelting, whether it comes from inside a factory or from used bottles and jars.

What happens to glass after it is recycled?

It is sorted, processed into cullet and melted with raw materials in a glass furnace to make new bottles, jars and other glass products.

Which glass cannot be recycled with bottles?

Heat-resistant cookware, ceramics, light bulbs and tube lights, mirrors, laminated or wired glass and old screens melt differently or carry contaminants, so they are kept out of container glass recycling.
