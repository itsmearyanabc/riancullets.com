Cullet / Amber

Amber glass cullet

Brown glass cullet, sorted by colour and processed for manufacturers of amber bottles and jars.

Amber glass is brown glass. Its colour filters out much of the ultraviolet and blue light that can spoil what is inside, which is why it is the standard choice for beer, medicines and many light-sensitive products.

Amber cullet is that glass recovered, sorted from clear and green, and processed so it can be remelted into new amber containers.

01

Where amber glass comes from

  • Beer bottles.
  • Pharmaceutical and medicine bottles.
  • Some spirit, sauce and cosmetic bottles.
  • Breakage and rejects from amber container production.
02

Why amber is kept separate

Amber is produced with a specific batch chemistry, and its colour has to stay consistent from one production run to the next. Clear or green glass mixed into amber cullet shifts that colour, so amber is sorted out of mixed glass and kept as its own stream.

Questions

Frequently asked

Why are beer and medicine bottles brown?

Amber glass blocks much of the ultraviolet light that can degrade beer and many medicines, so it protects the contents better than clear glass.

Can amber cullet be used to make clear glass?

No. Amber cullet would tint clear glass, so it is used for amber production.
