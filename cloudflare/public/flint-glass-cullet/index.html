Cullet / Flint

Flint glass cullet

Clear, colourless cullet kept apart from coloured glass, for manufacturers of clear bottles, jars and glassware.

In the glass trade, "flint" means clear, colourless soda-lime glass, the kind used for most transparent bottles and jars. Flint cullet, also called white cullet, is that glass recovered, sorted and processed for remelting.

It is the most demanding colour to supply. A clear furnace is very sensitive to colour: a small amount of amber or green glass in the batch is enough to tint the product. That is why flint cullet has to be sorted and kept apart with more care than any other colour.

01

Where flint cullet goes

  • Clear bottles for spirits, beverages and pharmaceuticals.
  • Food jars and clear containers.
  • Tableware and glassware.
  • Cosmetic and perfume bottles.

Flint is also the most flexible colour: clear cullet can be added to amber or green production, whereas coloured cullet cannot go the other way.

02

What buyers check

  • Colour: no amber or green glass mixed in.
  • Ceramics, stone and porcelain, which do not melt and cause defects in the finished glass.
  • Metals such as caps, rings and foil.
  • Organics: labels, paper, plastic and residue.
  • Glass type: heat-resistant and other non-soda-lime glass kept out.

Questions

Frequently asked

What is flint glass?

In glass manufacturing, flint is clear, colourless soda-lime glass. The term is not the same as "flint glass" in optics or lead crystal.

Why is flint cullet called white cullet?

Clear glass looks white when it is crushed, so the trade uses "white" and "flint" interchangeably for clear cullet.

Can flint cullet be used in coloured glass?

Yes. Clear cullet can go into amber or green production. Coloured cullet cannot go into clear glass without tinting it.
