Green glass cullet

Green cullet, sorted from mixed waste glass and processed for manufacturers of green bottles.

Green glass is used for wine, beer and many other beverage bottles. Green cullet is that glass recovered after use or from production, sorted by colour, and processed so it can be remelted into new green containers.

Green furnaces are generally the most tolerant of small amounts of other colours, which makes green cullet an important outlet for recovered glass. Consistency still matters: the closer the cullet is to the target shade, the easier it is for a plant to hold its colour.

Where green glass comes from

  • Wine and beer bottles.
  • Some soft drink, spirit and sauce bottles.
  • Breakage and rejects from green container production.
02

What makes green cullet usable

  • Sorted by colour, with clear and amber removed.
  • Free of ceramics, stone and porcelain.
  • Metals such as caps and rings removed.
  • Labels, paper and residue kept to a minimum.

Questions

Frequently asked

Why is green glass sorted separately?

Each colour is made with its own batch chemistry. Sorting keeps the colour of new glass consistent and lets each colour go back into the production that needs it.

Can mixed-colour glass be used in green production?

Green production can usually accept a small share of other colours, more than clear production can, but the exact tolerance is set by each manufacturer.
